Help & FAQ

Common questions, and how to reach us when the answer isn't here.

Getting started

Where do I begin?

Start small. Pick a folder of thirty or forty photos rather than a whole wedding for your first run, so you can see what PhotoFlow decides and how to adjust it. Once it feels predictable, point it at a full shoot.

Will it change or delete my original photos?

No. PhotoFlow reads your files and writes new output files. Your originals are never modified, moved or deleted. That said, keep your usual backups — good practice with any software.

Do my clients' photos get uploaded anywhere?

Processing happens on your own computer. PhotoFlow doesn't need an internet connection to sort photos, build albums, make ID sheets or create collages.

Albums

How many photos can it handle?

It's built for full Indian wedding volumes — many thousands of frames per event. Larger shoots take longer to analyse, and more memory and CPU cores help. If a particular folder size gives you trouble, please tell us the details.

It missed a face, or grouped two people together.

Face detection and person grouping are very good but not perfect — profiles, heavy shadow, sunglasses and very small distant faces are the usual causes. You can correct grouping and labels yourself. If you see a pattern of mistakes, send us an example; that's exactly the feedback that improves it.

Can I change the automatic layout?

Yes. The generated album is a starting point. You can change how many photos go on a spread, pick a different theme, and adjust individual spreads.

Passport & ID photos

Which size should I use?

Use the size the accepting authority asks for. PhotoFlow includes common presets and lets you type an exact size in millimetres. It helps you produce a photo at the size you specify — it doesn't certify that a photo will be accepted, so always check the current official requirements for the document in question.

Can I put more than one person on a sheet?

Yes. Add each person's photo to the sheet with their own crop and number of copies. It's designed for exactly this — a family applying together shouldn't need a separate sheet each.

The face enhancement looks too strong.

Each effect has its own slider, so turn down whichever is too much — skin smoothing is the usual culprit. Hold the Hold to see original button to compare against the untouched crop. You can also switch enhancement off entirely.

Collages

Which layout should I pick?

Leave it on Auto and it will choose based on your photos. If you'd rather decide: mosaic suits a mix of portrait and landscape shots, grid suits similar shapes, feature works when one photo carries the story, and masonry suits mostly-portrait sets.

It says a photo may look soft in print.

That means the photo has to be enlarged to fill its slot at the output size you've chosen. Either use a smaller output size, move that photo to a smaller slot, or swap in a higher-resolution version. The warning names the file and its effective resolution.

What are bleed and trim marks for?

They're for the print lab. Bleed extends the image slightly past where the paper will be cut, so a marginally off-centre cut doesn't leave a white edge. Trim marks show the cut line. Ask your lab whether they want them.

Installing and running

Windows warned me about the installer.

Beta builds aren't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn about an unrecognised app. Choose More info then Run anyway. Code signing is planned before the first paid release.

It feels slow on a big folder.

Analysing thousands of high-resolution frames is genuinely heavy work. More RAM and more CPU cores make a real difference. If something feels slower than it should — particularly if the window stops responding — please report it with the folder size and your PC specification.

How do I report a bug?

Use the contact page. The most useful reports include what you were doing, what you expected, what happened instead, and roughly how many photos were involved. Screenshots help a lot.
